# Event-isolated evaluation of cross-region landslide segmentation with compact models

*Working title. Authors and affiliations: to be added.*

## Abstract

*[DRAFT: to be rewritten when the pending experiments are finished.]*
Deep learning models for landslide mapping are usually trained and tested inside one benchmark, often with random tile splits, so
reported scores may reflect spatial or event leakage rather than the ability to map landslides in a new region. We assemble five public
landslide datasets (Landslide4Sense, HR-GLDD, GDCLD, LMHLD, CAS Landslide; 69,305 tiles after our conversion) and define
leave-one-dataset-out protocols in which every training tile that shares a documented physical event with the held-out dataset is
removed. Under this protocol, a compact SegFormer-B0 (3.7 M parameters) reaches landslide IoU between 22.1 and 36.7 on the held-out
datasets, against 40.9 in-dataset on GDCLD (RGB) and 39.2 on L4S (RGB only), and a ResNet34 U-Net with 6.6 times more parameters is worse on every
held-out dataset. A control that trains on the same number of tiles but keeps overlapping events shows that removing overlapping events lowers
landslide IoU by about 25 points on CAS and about 10 points on GDCLD, and by 3 points or less on the other three datasets. We also
document a channel-order error that, when uncorrected, collapses transfer to HR-GLDD (IoU 2.6 instead of 36.7). *[PENDING: band adapter,
LoRA teacher, distillation, calibration and CPU latency results, and multi-seed error bars.]*

## 1 Introduction

Automatic landslide mapping from optical satellite and UAV imagery is a standard task for rapid post-event response, and deep
learning now dominates it [S/A: see §2]. Most published results are obtained by training and testing on tiles cut from the same
imagery, frequently with random train/test splits. For example, the study that motivated this work trains and tests each of three
datasets separately with random 70/15/15 or 70/30 splits [1]. Such protocols cannot tell whether a model has learned landslides or the
appearance of one event, sensor and region, and neighbouring tiles from one event are strongly correlated. Recent work has begun to
evaluate on unseen events and regions [8, 9], but there is still no common event-isolated protocol over several public datasets, and
published numbers for the same model on the same dataset differ by several points between papers (§5.1).

We make the following contributions, each supported by experiments reported below.

1. **An event-isolated, multi-dataset evaluation protocol.** We convert five public datasets to one format, record which physical
   events each one contains, and define leave-one-dataset-out (B), event-isolated leave-one-dataset-out (B-strict) and
   leave-one-event/region-out (C) splits with an automatic leakage report (§3).
2. **A quantified effect of event overlap.** A size-matched control separates the effect of removing overlapping events from the effect
   of having less training data (§5.3).
3. **A fair baseline suite** (U-Net, DeepLabV3+, SegFormer-B0) re-run by us under identical settings, with the cost of each (§5.1, §5.2).
4. *[PENDING]* A sensor-agnostic input layer (band adapter), a LoRA-adapted SegFormer-B3 teacher distilled to SegFormer-B0, and
   calibration and CPU-latency measurements (§4, §5.4 to §5.6).
5. **Data-quality findings** that change results: the HR-GLDD array order, inconsistent counts and sensors between dataset
   documentation and files, and the absence of event identifiers in three of the five datasets (§3.3, §5.2).

We do not claim a new state-of-the-art score. All baselines are re-run; published numbers are quoted only where we read them in the
source and are labelled as such.

## 2 Related work

**Datasets.** Landslide4Sense (L4S) provides 128 x 128 Sentinel-2 + terrain patches [2]. GDCLD provides 15,621 train and validation tiles
of 1024 x 1024 RGB from nine earthquake events plus seven large test images from four events [3]. CAS Landslide offers satellite and UAV
imagery from nine regions [4, 20]. HR-GLDD offers 3 m PlanetScope patches from earthquake and rainfall events [5]. LMHLD offers 25,365
patches from five sensors and seven events [6]. Sen12Landslides adds Sentinel-1/2 time series [7] and was not used here.

**Foundation models and parameter-efficient adaptation.** Prithvi-EO-2.0 reports landslide results on L4S against U-Net baselines [11] and
a follow-up study compares it with SegFormer, SwinV2-B, SatMAE and TerraMind and examines band adaptation and geographic
generalisation [10]. A 2026 benchmark on GDCLD compares CNNs, transformers and foundation models with full fine-tuning, LoRA and AdaLoRA,
and reports up to 95% fewer trainable parameters at comparable accuracy [9]. A Clay-based hybrid with two-stage LoRA is evaluated on L4S [12].
These studies use one dataset (or one training distribution) and do not remove events shared between train and test sets.

**Cross-domain landslide mapping.** GeoPhysAdapter adapts a frozen vision foundation model with terrain, material and rainfall priors and
evaluates on an event-isolated benchmark built from four public sources [8]. Unsupervised domain adaptation has been applied to
landslide segmentation [13]. We do not use priors or target images; we ask how far compact supervised models transfer when
overlap is removed.

**Lightweight models and distillation.** Teacher-student distillation with quantisation has been reported for landslide extraction [14]
(known to us from an abstract-level snippet only). To our knowledge, a search in October 2026 found no study that combines
event-isolated multi-dataset evaluation, a sensor-agnostic input layer and edge-latency measurements, but this rests on a search and
not on exhaustive review; we will re-search before submission.

### 3.2 Protocols

**A (in-dataset).** The official split where one exists. CAS ships none; we use a deterministic region-level 70/15/15 split. LMHLD's
official split is a random tile split inside each event, and GDCLD's test events are probably also present in its training tiles;
protocol A numbers for these two datasets are therefore not event-isolated and are reported with this caveat.

**B (leave-one-dataset-out).** Test = all tiles of the held-out dataset; train and validation = the official train and validation tiles
of the other four (validation is used for checkpoint selection only).

**B-strict (event-isolated).** As B, but training tiles whose physical event also occurs in the held-out dataset are removed. Events
were assigned from the dataset papers and READMEs (`configs/dataset_events.json`); some assignments rely on place names and are
treated conservatively as the same event when plausible (e.g. Moxi town and Moxitaidi both as the 2022 Luding earthquake). For
datasets whose tiles carry no event label (L4S, HR-GLDD, GDCLD train/val) the whole dataset is dropped when any documented event
overlaps. B-strict therefore uses much less training data (5.5k to 25.0k tiles instead of 30.8k to 47.4k).

**C (leave-one-event/region-out).** Only where every tile has a known group: LMHLD (7 events) and CAS (10 regions). Validation is
one other whole group. *[PENDING: protocol C runs.]*

A leakage report checks, for every fold, overlapping tile references, groups straddling train and test, byte-identical tiles across
train and test, and documented events shared between train and test (all zero for B and B-strict apart from one near-black nodata
GDCLD crop; `splits/leakage_report.csv`). Hashing cannot detect overlapping neighbouring tiles; this is controlled only by splitting
at event or region level.

### 3.3 Data issues found

* **HR-GLDD channel order.** The paper describes the bands as red, green, blue, NIR, but the arrays appear to be B,G,R,NIR: mean
  channel values are 0.109 / 0.150 / 0.140 / 0.500, i.e. blue < red < green as in LMHLD's PlanetScope scenes whose order is documented
  as B,G,R,NIR, whereas R,G,B would make red darker than blue. We changed the mapping on this evidence before testing it. Rerunning
  the held-out HR-GLDD fold raised landslide IoU from 2.6 to 36.7 (SegFormer-B0) and from 2.2 to 29.9 (U-Net). The order is still not
  documented by the source and is described as "very likely".
* **LMHLD** is four-band (blue, green, red, NIR), not RGB; its Rio de Janeiro arrays carry a fifth channel that the paper does not
  explain (we drop it, assuming red edge).
* **CAS** documents 22,049 images in its README but ships 20,865; its README lists GF-1 for the Longxi River satellite subset while [1] describes the CAS images it used as GF-2; several zips are the same site imaged by different sensors, so we group
  by physical region.
* The Landslide4Sense Zenodo release includes masks for the test split, which the project README says are withheld.
* The PLoS study's "MS2LandsNet" data source is a re-upload of Landslide4Sense; MS2LandsNet is a network, not a dataset [1, 15].

## 4 Methods

**Models.** U-Net and DeepLabV3+ with an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet34 encoder, and SegFormer-B0 (MiT-B0 encoder, 3.7 M parameters),
all through the `segmentation_models_pytorch` library [V: Iakubovskii; SegFormer [V: Xie et al. 2021]; U-Net [V: Ronneberger et al. 2015];
DeepLabV3+ [V: Chen et al. 2018]]. Input is RGB for cross-dataset runs (the only modality all datasets share), all 14 bands for the
original L4S baselines.

**Training.** AdamW, cosine schedule, batch 32, mixed precision, loss = cross-entropy + soft Dice. In-dataset: 50 epochs (L4S), 20 epochs
(GDCLD). Cross-dataset: 10 epochs, random 128 px crops at native scale (so ground sampling is preserved when 128 px and 256 px datasets
are mixed), flips and 90-degree rotations. The weights of the epoch with the best validation landslide IoU are used once on the test
set. In cross-dataset runs the per-epoch validation set is a random subset of at most 3,000 tiles; the test set is never subsampled.

**Metrics.** Landslide IoU, mIoU (mean of landslide and background IoU), F1, precision and recall at threshold 0.5, PR-AUC (histogram
estimate over 2,000 probability bins) and expected calibration error (ECE; 15 confidence bins on the winning class), accumulated over
all test pixels of a held-out dataset.

**Band adapter.** A 1 x 1 convolution maps an 11-slot channel bank (R,G,B,NIR,red edge,SWIR1,SWIR2,slope,DEM,VV,VH) to three channels
in front of a pretrained 3-channel network. It is initialised as the identity on R,G,B and zero elsewhere, so training starts exactly at
the RGB model; unavailable slots are masked, and non-RGB slots are randomly dropped during training (per sample with probability 0.3,
per slot 0.2) so that the model does not depend on bands that a target sensor lacks. L4S fills 9 of the 11 slots (no SAR).

**Teacher and distillation.** *[Implemented, not yet run at scale.]* A SegFormer-B3 teacher is adapted with LoRA (rank r on the attention
q, kv, proj and MLP fc1, fc2 projections; base weights frozen; decoder and adapter trained) [V: Hu et al. 2022], then frozen; the SegFormer-B0
student minimises (1 - a) (CE + Dice) + a T^2 KL(teacher_T || student_T) with a = 0.5, T = 2 [V: Hinton et al. 2015].

**Reproducibility.** Code, configs, splits, leakage report and every run's `result.json` (config, seed, git revision, library versions,
per-epoch history, test metrics including per-event breakdowns) are in the project repository. All reported training used a single
Kaggle Tesla T4 per job. Weekly GPU time was capped at 30 h; the log is in `docs/gpu_budget.md`.

## 5 Experiments

### 5.1 In-dataset baselines (protocol A)

[Table 2: generated; see `results/tables/paper_tables.md`.] On L4S (all 14 bands, 800 test tiles) the U-Net reaches mIoU 73.3 / F1 64.9,
SegFormer-B0 72.6 / 63.7 and DeepLabV3+ 71.9 / 62.3; with RGB only, SegFormer-B0 and U-Net fall to landslide IoU 39.2 and 37.9 (from 46.7 and
48.0). For reference, we read U-Net mIoU/F1 of 70.4 / 59.7 [11, Table VII; 6-band input] and 70.44 / 59.66 [10, Table 3] on L4S; our
higher values use different inputs, encoder, loss and checkpoint selection and should not be read as an improvement over those papers.
On GDCLD (256 px, official test crops) SegFormer-B0 is best (mIoU 70.1, landslide IoU 40.9) with 6.6 times fewer parameters and 4.6 times
fewer FLOPs than U-Net (68.96 / 38.7). Our GDCLD scores are well below the 85.06% SegFormer mIoU quoted for the dataset paper in our
planning notes (not read by us); training longer (60 epochs) did not help (test mIoU 69.6) and the test crops contain only 1.06% landslide
pixels against about 10% in train and validation. We could not explain the gap and report it as a limitation. Validation IoU (0.70)
substantially overstates test IoU (0.35 to 0.41), consistent with validation tiles sharing events with training tiles.

### 5.2 Cross-dataset transfer (protocol B-strict)

[Tables 3 to 5: generated.] Under B-strict the SegFormer-B0 landslide IoU is 24.1 (L4S), 36.7 (HR-GLDD), 28.6 (GDCLD), 22.8 (LMHLD) and 22.1 (CAS);
the U-Net obtains 19.5, 29.9, 17.5, 18.2 and 2.3. SegFormer-B0 is ahead on all five held-out datasets. ECE is below 0.06 on four of five held-out datasets for SegFormer-B0 but 0.108 on CAS, and 0.178 for the U-Net on LMHLD: models are over-confident on unfamiliar sensors.
Per-region results (seed 0, size-matched run) show that failures are local: on CAS, Palu (1.9) and Lombok (9.6) against Jiuzhai valley
(55.5); on GDCLD, Mesetas (2.2) and Lushan (13.2) against Palu and Sumatra (about 31); on LMHLD, Wenchuan/IKONOS (4.5) against 19 to 33
elsewhere. All cross-dataset values so far are single seeds, so differences of 2 to 3 points are within the noise we expect but have not
yet measured. *[PENDING: three seeds.]*

### 5.3 Effect of event overlap

[Table 6: generated.] Plain protocol B (events may overlap) gives SegFormer-B0 landslide IoU of 48.3 on CAS and 45.3 on GDCLD, against 22.1
and 28.6 under B-strict. Because B-strict also has less training data, we repeated plain B with the training set randomly cut to the
B-strict size: CAS stays at 46.7 (so the drop is due to removing shared events, not data volume), GDCLD falls to 38.9 (about 6 points
data volume, about 10 points overlap), and L4S, LMHLD and HR-GLDD change by 3 points or less (24.1 vs 22.8, 22.8 vs 19.4, 36.7 vs 38.1).
Event overlap therefore inflates the cross-dataset score substantially on two of five datasets and negligibly on the rest. The plain-B
runs for HR-GLDD were made with the wrong channel order and are not reported.

## 6 Limitations

* Most cross-dataset results are single-seed; we have not yet measured seed-to-seed variation.
* Cross-dataset training uses RGB (or the adapter's subset of bands), 128 px crops and 10 epochs; longer training and larger models were
  not explored at scale. GDCLD and CAS were downscaled by 4 and 2; fine detail visible at 0.2 to 1 m resolution is lost.
* B-strict event assignments are partly inferred from place names; residual overlap cannot be excluded. Event identifiers are missing
  for L4S, HR-GLDD and GDCLD train/val, so those datasets can only be held out whole.
* Our GDCLD scores are far below a published value that we have not read (85.06% mIoU); the gap is unexplained.
* HR-GLDD band order and LMHLD's fifth Rio channel are inferred, not documented.
* CAS has no negative tiles; false-alarm statistics are unavailable there. ECE is computed pixel-wise and is dominated by background.
* Foundation models (Prithvi, TerraFM) were not evaluated because of the 30 GPU-hour weekly budget; this is a limitation of scope, not a
  finding.
* No edge-device (Jetson) measurements; CPU measurements only *[PENDING]*.
